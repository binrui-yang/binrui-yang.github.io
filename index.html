Working Papers

Matching Diverse Types with Axel Anderson, 2026

Q: If team coordination benefits from diversity in matched types, what can we say about equilibrium matchings and payoffs?
A: Quite a lot, from the single assumption we call preferences for diversity: pushing partners’ types further apart expands what a pair can achieve. This simple first-order monotonicity condition does two things for certain. It confines equilibrium matches to two “negative quadrants,” with low types matching up and high types matching down, so no one matches a clone. And it makes the most extreme types the biggest winners, so payoffs are U-shaped. In unbalanced markets, it also uniquely pins down the set of unmatched types, and with it the exact quadrants where matches can form. What it does not pin down is sorting within each quadrant, which depends on complementarity and transferability (Legros and Newman, 2007). Yet we show that in a broad class of environments, matching to play repeated games, opposites do attract: types with opposite time preferences pair off neatly.
